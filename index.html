Build your first week of running

Running looks like one of the cheapest, easiest sports to start: no court, no club, no gear beyond shoes. That's true, but it hides how much there still is to learn about training. Even a beginner's first week already involves decisions — not because running is dangerous, but because going gradually and sustainably takes a plan, not just willpower.

    Novices get hurt more often

    New runners are injured roughly twice as often, per hour of running, as people who've been running for a while — a rate averaged across study populations, not a personal prediction. It's part of why building up gradually is worth taking seriously.

    Overuse injuries, not joint damage

    The knee, lower leg and ankle/foot are the most commonly affected areas — but these are overwhelmingly overuse conditions like shin splints and tendinopathy, not evidence that running wears down healthy joints long-term. That's a separate question this plan doesn't try to answer.

    Built for gradual progress

    How fast weekly running volume increases shows an association with injury risk in the research, though that evidence is rated low-quality and meant to be treated cautiously rather than as a firm rule. Recognised programmes like the NHS's Couch to 5K increase running time slowly, add a rest day between every run, and treat warm-up and cool-down as standard — the plan above spreads rest the same way once you choose how many days to run.
